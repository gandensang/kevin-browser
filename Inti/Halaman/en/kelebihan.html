Pros & cons

This browser is made for one thing: saving memory. There is a price to pay for that, and all of it is written down here.

Pros

  • Light on memory. See the numbers below.
  • Sleeping tabs. Only 2–4 tabs stay awake at once, depending on the laptop's RAM (2 tabs for 2 GB of RAM). The tab left unseen the longest goes to sleep, and so does any tab not seen for 5 minutes, and every background tab when the laptop is almost out of memory. A sleeping tab loads again when opened. WhatsApp Web, tabs playing sound, and tabs with unsent form input are never put to sleep.
  • Tabs are not lost. Open tabs come back, asleep, the next time the browser is opened, even after the laptop shuts down suddenly. A tab closed by accident comes back with Ctrl+Shift+T.
  • Background tabs are not loaded until opened. Opening many links at once stays light.
  • Lightweight YouTube. YouTube opens in its mobile version, which uses half the memory of the desktop version. Music keeps playing when you switch tabs. If the laptop's graphics card can do it, videos are decoded by the graphics card, so the processor works less.
  • Videos don't play by themselves, YouTube included. A video only plays after it is clicked. On one detik.com video page, this saves about 800 MB of memory. Only WhatsApp Web is an exception.
  • The modern web still works. The engine is WebKit, the engine also used by Safari. JavaScript, YouTube, and Google work as usual.
  • Ads and trackers are blocked. News sites become much lighter, sometimes half (see the numbers below). The block lists are updated once a week. Tracking parameters in addresses, such as utm_source and fbclid, are removed too.
  • Web pages are fenced in. Every page runs inside a sandbox, separate from the files on the laptop.
  • One 4.9 MB file, with no need to install .NET.

Cons

  • Blocked ads can leave empty space. The ads are blocked, but the boxes they would sit in are not hidden yet.
  • YouTube video ads still appear, because they come from YouTube's own servers.
  • The block lists use about 60 MB of disk. Once a week the lists are downloaded again (about 4 MB). For about 10 seconds at that time, memory use grows by up to about 380 MB.
  • A sleeping tab has to load again when it is opened, so there is a short wait.
  • Video calls don't work yet, including Google Meet, Zoom, and WhatsApp calls. The web engine that Linux Mint provides is built without the video-call feature (WebRTC). The camera and microphone are only allowed for WhatsApp Web, for voice messages. Notifications are also only for WhatsApp Web.
  • YouTube looks like it does on a tablet (the mobile version), to save memory. The desktop version can still be chosen, see the Guide. Even so, YouTube is still the heaviest page: about 540 MB.
  • Videos are 720p at most, so the laptop is not overloaded.
  • Videos have to be clicked first, including on YouTube and the video feeds of social media. Only WhatsApp Web is an exception.
  • Not Chrome. A small number of sites are made specifically for Chrome and might not display perfectly.
  • Linux only, for now. There is no Windows version yet.
